Pixel-art sprite sheet: a 4-frame spell-casting animation of the ACOLYTE, a hooded cultist spellcaster, for a Doom/Hexen-style first-person game where monsters are drawn as sprites from several camera angles. The acolyte gathers fire in one hand and hurls it at the player as a fireball.

REFERENCE IMAGES, in the order attached:
1. The acolyte's turnaround sheet (front, front three-quarter, side, rear three-quarter, back). It is the character design: the oxblood robe darkening to soot at the ragged hem, the tarnished bronze rune trim and shoulder mantle, the tall pointed cowl with a black face and two ember-orange eyes, the bony ash-grey hands, the rope belt with bone charms, the bronze censer on its chain, and the shading style.
2. The acolyte standing, seen from THIS sheet's camera view. Match its camera angle, the way it faces, its pixel size, outline weight, scale and ground line exactly.

The CASTING HAND is the empty hand with the ember glow in its palm in reference 2. The other hand keeps holding the censer by its chain in every frame; the censer just hangs and sways a little. Never swap hands, and never draw a third hand.

LAYOUT
- One horizontal row of 4 equal cells, read left to right, each cell as wide as reference 2.
- Background: flat pure magenta (#FF00FF) everywhere, with no gradient, texture, vignette, glow, cast shadow or floor.
- The cells are separated by thin vertical lines of pure cyan (#00FFFF), each running the full height of the image.
- The acolyte is drawn at the same scale in all 4 cells, exactly as tall as in reference 2.
- The hem rests on the same ground line in every cell, at the same height as in reference 2. The acolyte does not hop, float or step.
- The tip of the cowl is the highest point of the figure in every frame: the casting hand and the fire never rise above it.
- The whole figure, including the outstretched hand, the fire and the censer, fits inside each cell with a clear magenta margin. Nothing touches a cell edge or a cyan line, and nothing is cropped.
- No text, labels, numbers, frame borders, speed lines, motion blur, smoke, sparks, flying embers or a fireball in flight. The only fire is in the casting hand, as described below.
- The fire in the hand is a solid shape with hard pixel edges: a bright yellow core, then orange, then a deep red rim. No glow, haze or light spilling onto the magenta around it.
- Crisp pixel art with hard edges and no anti-aliasing against the magenta. No semi-transparent pixels, and no pink or purple glow bleeding into the background.

THE CAST
- Frame 1, WIND-UP: the casting arm draws up and back, and the hand comes up beside the cowl at head height, palm turned up, fingers curled like claws. A small flame, the size of a fist, kindles in the palm. The body leans back a little and the cowl tilts back slightly.
- Frame 2, RAISED HAND: the hand is held high beside the cowl at eye height, fingers spread around a bright ball of fire as large as the acolyte's head. The body leans back the most, the casting shoulder raised. The eyes burn brighter.
- Frame 3, RELEASE: the instant the fireball has been hurled. The casting arm is thrust straight out the way the acolyte faces, at chest height, the palm open and pushed forward, the fingers splayed. The fire has left the hand; only a small orange glow remains in the palm. The body leans forward into the throw and the cowl dips forward.
- Frame 4, RECOVER: back in the standing pose of reference 2, the casting hand at waist height with its faint ember glow.
