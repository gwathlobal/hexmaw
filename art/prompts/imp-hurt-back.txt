
VIEW: directly from behind, as in the fifth cell of reference 1 and in reference 2. We see the imp's back, the back of its head, horns and ears; the face is hidden in both frames. Keep this angle: the body must not turn toward the viewer.
- Frame 1: the hit comes from the far side, so the imp is thrown back toward the viewer. The head snaps back, tipping the horns and the back of the skull toward us and showing the top of the head. The back arches, the shoulder blades squeeze together, and both arms fly up and out to image-left and image-right at shoulder height, claws splayed. Knees buckle, feet planted. The tail, in full view, whips up stiff.
- Frame 2: the imp doubles over away from the viewer; the rounded back fills the frame and the bowed head sinks between the hunched shoulders, so only the horns and ear tips show above them. The arm on image-right is pulled in front of the body (clutching the chest, hidden); the arm on image-left dangles. Knees deeply bent. The tail droops low.
