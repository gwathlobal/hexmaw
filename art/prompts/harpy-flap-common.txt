Pixel-art sprite sheet: the HOVERING WINGBEAT of the HARPY, a winged hag that circles under a dungeon's ceiling, for a Doom/Hexen-style first-person game where monsters are drawn as sprites from several camera angles.

REFERENCE IMAGES, in the order attached:
1. The harpy's turnaround sheet (front, front three-quarter, side, rear three-quarter, back). It is the character design: a gaunt hag with grey leathery skin, a hooked, beak-like nose, a shrieking mouth of small yellowed teeth, glowing sickly yellow eyes and long matted ash-grey hair; a body of ragged slate and soot-brown feathers; ARMS THAT ARE WINGS, with three long, bony, rust-clawed fingers at each wing's bend; scaly dark bird legs with big hooked rust-coloured talons; a short fan of tail feathers. Also the shading style.
2. The harpy hovering, seen from THIS sheet's camera view. Match its camera angle, the way it faces, its pixel size, outline weight and scale exactly.

The harpy has exactly two wings and two legs in every frame, and no human arms or hands besides the clawed fingers at the wings' bends. The face, hair, eyes and talons look the same in every frame.

LAYOUT
- A 2 x 2 grid of 4 equal cells, each twice as wide as it is tall: frames 1 and 2 in the top row (left to right), frames 3 and 4 in the bottom row (left to right).
- Background: flat pure magenta (#FF00FF) everywhere, with no gradient, texture, vignette, glow, cast shadow or floor.
- The cells are separated by thin lines of pure cyan (#00FFFF): one vertical line down the middle of the image, one horizontal line across it, each running the full width or height of the image.
- The harpy is drawn at the same scale in every cell: the body, head and legs exactly as big in every frame, the harpy from wingtips to talons in frame 1 about three quarters of the cell's height. The wide cells leave room for the spread wings; the wings NEVER cross a cyan line.
- The talons hang at about the same height in every cell; the body bobs only a little.
- The whole figure, including both wingtips and the talons, fits inside each cell with a clear magenta margin. Nothing touches a cell edge or a cyan line, and nothing is cropped.
- No text, labels, numbers, frame borders, speed lines, motion blur, smear trails, loose feathers, dust or effects. Only the harpy.
- Crisp pixel art with hard edges and no anti-aliasing against the magenta. No semi-transparent pixels, and no pink or purple glow bleeding into the background.

THE WINGBEAT: a heavy, labouring flap that holds the harpy in the air in one place. The body stays upright, the legs hang below with the talons half-open, the head shrieks forward. The four frames are four clearly different wing positions:
- Frame 1 (top left), TOP: exactly reference 2's pose: the wings raised high above the shoulders in a V, their tips above the head.
- Frame 2 (top right), DOWNSTROKE: the wings swept out to the sides, straight and fully spread, level with the shoulders, the feathers fanned; the body lifted a little.
- Frame 3 (bottom left), BOTTOM: the wings pushed down below the shoulders, their tips down beside the legs, the feathers pressed together; the body at its highest.
- Frame 4 (bottom right), UPSTROKE: the wings folded in and drawn up, sharply bent: the bends with their clawed fingers raised to the height of the head, close beside it, the long feathers hanging straight down from them, so the wings are at their narrowest of all four frames; the body sinking back. Clearly different from frame 1, whose wings are spread in a V.
The four frames loop: after frame 4 comes frame 1 again.
